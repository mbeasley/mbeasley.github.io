Basic Internet Security

February 2, 2026

Note: I wrote this for non-technical friends and family. I’m not a security expert, but I’m often asked for baseline advice that can make people significantly safer online.

the short version

  1. Don’t reuse passwords between accounts
  2. Use a Password Manager (like 1Password) to generate and store long and unique passwords for each account.
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) anywhere that you can.

That’s it. Keep reading for the longer version.

Most account “hacks” happen for one reason: the same password is reused across many sites.

When a company’s data is breached, attackers may get email addresses and passwords. They then try those same passwords on email, banking, shopping, and social media accounts. This works far more often than people expect.

You can check whether your email has appeared in known breaches at haveibeenpwned.com. If it has, that does not mean you did anything wrong. It means a company you trusted failed to protect its data.

what to do about it

Use strong and unique passwords combined with two-factor authentication (2FA). That’s it. Doing so will make you significantly safer on the internet.

A strong password is:

  • long (> 16 characters, ideally longer)
  • complex
  • impersonal (no dates, initials, names, nicknames, etc)

Length is more important than complexity:

  • uP4d*83 ← cracked in minutes
  • ball coffee charter kidnap outlet ← cracked in centuries

Don’t Reuse Passwords

Never reuse passwords for:

  • email: If someone gets your email, they can reset almost everything else.
  • money: Banks, PayPal, Venmo, taxes, investments.
  • shopping: Amazon or anywhere your credit card is stored.
  • social media: These accounts are often used to scam people who trust you or to gain access to more important accounts.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

2FA adds a second check after your password, usually using something you have. Even if your password is stolen, your account stays protected.

From best to worst:

  • hardware security key (e.g., YubiKey)
  • app-based phone key (push notification)
  • authenticator app code
  • SMS text message
  • email code

Enable 2FA everywhere it’s available. Be cautious with services that don’t offer it.

Password Managers

A Password Manager makes strong, unique passwords easy.

What it does:

  • stores passwords securely
  • generates long, unique passwords for every site
  • autofills passwords on your phone and computer

Important note: your Password Manager is extremely sensitive. Protect it with a very strong master password and 2FA.

Recommendation: 1Password

Non-technical alternative: pen and paper, as long as passwords are long and never reused.

Additional Practices

  • keep your phone, computer, and browser up to date
  • use a strong passcode on your devices

Read more tips at 1Password.com.

Final Advice

Don’t try to fix everything at once. Start first with email, then banking, shopping, social media, and so on.
